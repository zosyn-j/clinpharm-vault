# CSU Phase 2 population comparison

This page is a conservative, source-backed comparison of the enrolled **CSU Phase 2 populations** across the main public competitor programs currently represented in the vault. It is meant to answer a practical question: **were these studies really enrolling the same kind of patients, or only broadly similar ones?**

## Bottom line

The Phase 2 CSU programs were **directionally similar**, meaning they generally enrolled **adult patients with active CSU that remained inadequately controlled on H1-antihistamines**. But they were **not literally identical populations**.

The biggest practical differences in the current local source layer are:
- **prior biologic exposure handling**, especially around omalizumab
- **minimum CSU diagnosis duration**
- **whether symptom-duration and H1 stability wording were explicit**
- **how CIndU / physical urticaria was excluded or carved out**
- **how much operational detail is public**, including geography / site-count context

## Practical read

- **Closest pair by overall population shape:** remibrutinib and barzolvolimab still look the most similar on the current public layer, with rilzabrutinib clearly in the same broad adult active-antihistamine-refractory CSU bucket.
- **Most important non-equivalence issue remains prior omalizumab handling.** The richer biologic-exposure column makes that clearer than the older simple Oma gate Y/N.
- **Best public operational detail:** rilzabrutinib and fenebrutinib currently have the strongest site-count / geography detail in the local source layer.
- **Most explicit H1 stability wording:** barzolvolimab, fenebrutinib, and EVO756.
- **Most explicit physical-urticaria exclusion wording:** remibrutinib and fenebrutinib.
- **Most permissive currently visible omalizumab stance:** remibrutinib and EVO756; **most restrictive:** fenebrutinib.
- **Most publication-thin public population layer:** EVO756 still looks broadly comparable, but several operational and exclusion details remain thinner than the more mature BTK and KIT programs.
