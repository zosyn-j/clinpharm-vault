CSU Phase 2 Study Design and Key Eligibility

Background

Randomized Placebo-controlled Phase 2 Study

• Mast cells (MCs) are key effector cells in chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU).
• Barzolvolimab, a humanized monoclonal antibody that inhibits KIT activation by SCF,
demonstrated rapid and durable depletion of skin MCs1.
• We previously reported that barzolvolimab treatment resulted in statistically significant
and clinically meaningful improvement in weekly Urticaria Activity Score (UAS7) at 12
weeks, with deepening of response over 52 weeks in antihistamine-refractory CSU
patients (NCT05368285), including patients who received prior omalizumab.
• Low circulating IgE levels have been associated with autoimmune (Type 2b) disease
and refractoriness to anti-IgE therapies2,3,4.
• Here we report UAS7 and Urticaria Control Test over 7 days (UCT7) results in relation to
baseline total IgE level at Weeks 12 and 52 for the two higher barzolvolimab doses.

Conclusions

• Barzolvolimab treatment demonstrated rapid and strong efficacy in a Phase 2 study in CSU and was well tolerated.
• Similarly strong efficacy was observed in patients with normal/high (≥ 40 IU/mL) or low (< 40 IU/mL) IgE levels for both dosing regimens as determined
by:
• Changes in UAS7 or UCT7 at Weeks 12 and 52
• Rates of well-controlled or complete disease control

• These data suggest that mast cells are important drivers of CSU regardless of the underlying endotype and are consistent with the mechanism of
action of barzolvolimab.
• Barzolvolimab is a promising therapy for all patients with moderate to severe CSU, regardless of underlying endotype.
